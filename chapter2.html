CHAPTER II

Grass and Gossips

✦ ───── ❦ ───── ✦

A week apart, a first flight, and one night by the river that made the world feel a little less distant.

A Week Apart

Finally, we had a week of the Schlumberger training. And just like that, there was distance between us again. You were in a different group, with Kedar and the others, while I was with Ramanan. We were in different places, following different schedules, barely getting to see each other.

The days were moving so fast. I liked the training, but a part of me wished I had been in your group. Of course, I knew that was too much to ask. We had known each other for barely a few weeks, and here I was, already wishing for more time around you.

I was curious about you. Interested in knowing you better. But I also knew my limits. I was in a relationship, and no matter what I felt, I wanted to be careful about how I acted. I didn't want my curiosity to become someone's discomfort, or my feelings to become a complication in your life.

So I kept going, telling myself to enjoy the training and not think too much. The week passed before I could even understand where the time had gone.

And then came the trip. I was excited to travel with everyone, but more than that, I was looking forward to spending time with you. I wanted to know more about you, hear more of your stories, and discover the little things that made you who you were. At the same time, I kept reminding myself not to bother you too much.

My First Flight

Funny enough, this was going to be my first flight ever. I had never flown before, and I was nervous about all the little things I didn't know. I wished you were there to guide me, explain things to me, and make me feel a little safer through an experience that was completely new to me.

And then you did something that I still remember fondly. You asked if we could sit together and do the web check-in. I didn't even know I had to do a web check-in!

Me, Ramanan, and you sat together and figured it out. You got the window seat, but you were ready to give it up for me. I had known you for only about two weeks, and yet you were willing to let me have something you could have enjoyed yourself.

Sometimes, the smallest gestures make a person feel unexpectedly special. ♡

I was so happy. I would have been perfectly content just sitting beside you and enjoying the view, but the fact that you were willing to give up your window seat meant a lot to me.

You even thought about how I might feel nauseous. You got cotton for my ears so I wouldn't have to deal with that uncomfortable tingling feeling during take-off. You were thinking about things I hadn't even figured out how to ask about yet.

We had a nice conversation during the flight. With three people together, Ramanan was doing what Ramanan does best: making jokes at my expense. We laughed, talked, and eventually fell asleep for a while.

Before I knew it, the flight had landed. Another new experience had passed in what felt like no time at all.

The Seat Beside You

We got on the bus, and luckily, I got to sit beside you. I remember looking around, wondering whether someone else would take that seat before I could. I was hoping to sit with you, but I didn't want to make it obvious or turn it into a whole thing.

I don't even remember exactly how it happened. Maybe I rushed in. Maybe I somehow managed to get there before everyone else. Whatever happened, I ended up beside you, and I was happy about it.

We started talking about college. We were passing through Surat, and somehow the conversation brought back memories of NIT Warangal and NIT Calicut. We compared our experiences, talked about college life, and slowly slipped into the kind of conversation where you don't notice how much time has passed.

At lunchtime, our group sat down to find something to eat. There was this little food desk with cookies, berries, and other things. And then I saw you getting excited over the chocolate-chip cookies, trying to figure out whether we could get them.

I don't know why that little moment stayed with me. Maybe it was because you looked like a small child excited about a simple thing. There was something so genuine and cute about it.

I liked seeing that side of you. Not the serious person in training, not the person who seemed so composed around everyone, but someone who could get excited over a small thing like cookies.

And, somehow, I became even more curious about you.

Things with Nikki were not going particularly well. I was feeling lonely at times and wanted to share what was happening in my life. But I kept questioning myself: was it okay to tell you these things, or was I just finding another reason to get your attention?

I was trying to be moral, but I was also trying to be honest with myself. I wanted a friend to talk to, yet I was afraid of what my own intentions might look like. So I kept going back and forth in my head, trying to find a line that felt right.

Half a Lesson, Half a You

Then came the L&T training. It was a good experience, and we were all settling into the routine. During the breaks, I kept hoping I might get to sit beside you.

Sometimes you would get into the classroom first, and I would end up sitting with Ramanan in the second row. I would wait, watching how you approached the lesson, how you read things, and how seriously you took it all.

I was probably paying half my attention to the lesson and the other half to you.

It wasn't because I had some elaborate plan or some intention I was trying to hide. At least, that was how I understood it then. I was simply curious. I had never met someone quite like you, and I wanted to understand you better.

The days kept moving. Training, meals, conversations, and all the little moments in between. I didn't know that one ordinary night would become one of the memories I would return to most often.

Grass and Gossips

One day in Surat, I was feeling particularly low. Everyone seemed to have their own plans. Some people were going out for drinks, and I think Kedar went with Jessica that evening.

We went for dinner, and eventually Shreyash and Ramanan left to sleep. And then, somehow, it was just you and me.

I think that was the night something changed in the way we understood each other.

We went outside into the lawn and started walking. There was a river flowing beside us, grass beneath our feet, and a little dew settling over everything as the night grew quieter.

For once, we started talking without trying so hard to be careful. We spoke about our hearts, our relationships, what it meant to have a partner, and what we thought love was supposed to feel like.

I didn't stop to think about whether my answers sounded impressive. I wasn't trying to impress you. I just said whatever came into my mind, honestly and without dressing it up. And you did the same.

Slowly, we sat down in the grass. At some point, the conversation became so comfortable that we started rolling around in the grass like absolute idiots.

Well, I think I rolled around by myself for a while until someone came along and embarrassed both of us into going back to our rooms.

I remember wishing we could somehow continue the conversation in the room. But then I thought about the boundaries I had been trying so hard to respect. I didn't want to overstep or make you uncomfortable, so I backed off.

Still, that night remains one of the best days I remember from the entire experience.

I remember the grass, the night sky, the stars, and the sound of the river flowing beside us. It was such a simple setting, yet I felt lighter than I had in a long time.

There was no grand event, no carefully planned moment, no big declaration. Just two people talking, sharing things they might not normally share, and finding comfort in the conversation.

Some nights stay with you long after the night is over. ♡

The Call I Almost Didn't Make

The next day, in the middle of the night, I found myself feeling overwhelmed again. I wanted to talk to you, but I hesitated. Twice, I think, I stopped myself from calling.

I kept wondering whether I was asking for too much, whether I was crossing a line, and whether I should simply handle things on my own.

But eventually, I called.

I cried while talking to you. And you told me that you wished you could help me.

It might sound like a simple sentence, but it comforted me more than you probably realized. You didn't try to take control of my situation, tell me what I should do, or cross a boundary just because I was upset.

You simply showed empathy.

Until then, I don't think I had fully understood that someone could comfort another person so sincerely while still respecting their own principles and boundaries. You made me see a different way of dealing with people.

I admired that about you. I admired the way you handled the situation, the way you listened, and the way you could be kind without making things more complicated than they needed to be.

I think that was also when you mentioned that I was like a brother to you.

I won't lie. A little part of me was offended. I remember thinking, "I'm not, though."

But I also knew that not every thought needs to be said immediately. Maybe some conversations need the right time, and maybe some things are better understood slowly.

So I let it be for the moment.

The Charm I Wanted to Learn

After that, things slowly started feeling better. We were getting closer, and the days seemed to have their own rhythm. We were always running late, making jokes, and finding little reasons to talk.

I noticed something about you: people seemed to give you attention without you even asking for it. You had this charm that made people feel drawn to you, that changed the way they looked at things around you.

I really liked that quality. More than that, I wanted to learn from it. I wanted to understand how you could be so naturally yourself and still make people feel seen and comfortable.

I admired you, and I wanted to become a better person myself. Someone who could listen better, understand people better, and be more thoughtful about how I treated them.

A Poem on the Way Back

I still remember the last day, when we were getting back on the same bus. You started telling me about your hobbies and the things you had written.

And then I saw the poem you had written in Surat, the one where you combined chemistry with poetry.

Trust me, I don't think I have ever admired someone's creativity quite like that. The way you connected two completely different things and turned them into something artistic was beautiful to me.

I don't know why you left it behind, whether you lost interest or simply kept it as a hobby. But I was genuinely mesmerized by what you had created.

I wanted to tell you that I was a writer too. But looking at your work, I found myself wondering whether I had ever really seen writing used in such a beautiful way.

You weren't just putting words together. You were connecting ideas, finding meaning between them, and turning that connection into something that felt entirely your own.

I remember thinking, "Oh my God, this is someone I could learn so much from."

Not because I wanted to become you, but because being around someone like you made me want to grow. I wanted to become a better writer, a better friend, and perhaps a person who could look at the world with a little more depth.

What Surat Left Behind

Surat became more than just another stop in the training. It gave us time to talk, to share parts of ourselves, and to understand each other a little better.

There was the first flight, the bus ride, the chocolate-chip cookies, the training sessions where I paid half my attention to you, and the night beside the river.

There was the phone call when I cried, the comfort you offered without crossing a line, the jokes, the late days, and the poem that showed me a side of you I hadn't known before.

I had arrived curious about you. I left Surat knowing that I admired you, trusted you a little more, and wanted to keep discovering who you were.

I still didn't have all the answers. I was still figuring out my feelings, my boundaries, and the things I needed to understand about myself.

But I knew that these memories mattered to me. And for the first time, I felt that perhaps getting to know someone could change the way you see yourself, too.

Surat happened. And somewhere between the grass, the gossips, and the stars, we became a little less like strangers. ♡
